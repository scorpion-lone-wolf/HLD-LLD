# Curriculum Research Notes

The curriculum was revised after reviewing recent public interview reports and interview-preparation material.

## Recurring signals

### LLD / machine coding
Recent reports commonly emphasize:
- fully runnable code;
- requirement clarification before coding;
- classes/separation of concerns;
- extensibility;
- edge cases and validations;
- tests/demo;
- concurrency follow-ups;
- ability to explain and modify the submitted design.

Examples reviewed include recent Flipkart SDE-2 machine-coding/LLD reports and a Razorpay SDE-II ATM LLD report.

### HLD / design
Recurring expectations include:
- functional and non-functional requirements;
- API and data model/schema;
- architecture built from requirements rather than technology name-dropping;
- deep dives into bottlenecks;
- consistency, concurrency and failure handling;
- trade-offs;
- project/production-system discussion at experienced levels.

## Curriculum implications

1. Patterns are taught as tools, not a memorization syllabus.
2. Runnable TypeScript and tests are first-class LLD skills.
3. Requirements/API/schema are taught before architecture.
4. Concurrency and idempotency appear in both LLD and HLD.
5. Reliability and observability are mandatory, not optional polish.
6. Case studies increase in difficulty and introduce concepts only after prerequisites.
7. Resume-based design is a separate interview skill.
8. Progress requires evidence through mastery gates.
